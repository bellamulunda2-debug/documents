A Trace is your daily proof of work — a timestamped record of what you actually did. Not what you planned to do, not what you're thinking about doing, but what you shipped, learned, or pushed forward today. Every trace you publish feeds your Trajectory and tells the Xera community — and anyone viewing your public profile — that you're a builder who shows up.

## What Goes in a Trace

A trace is flexible enough to capture any kind of progress. You can include:

- **Text** — Describe what you worked on, what you learned, or what you figured out
- **Images** — Screenshots, designs, sketches, or visual proof of your work
- **Videos** — Short demos, walkthroughs, or recordings of your output
- **Metrics / Numbers** — Quantified progress like lines of code, words written, reps completed, or revenue earned
- **Progress Percentage** — A slider showing how far through your linked ARC milestone you are

The best traces combine several of these: a short written description, a screenshot or screen recording, and a concrete metric. Show, don't just tell.

## Creating a Trace

You can log a trace in under two minutes. Use the **+** button from anywhere in the app.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap + Create">
    Press the **+** button in the navigation bar and select **Trace** from the content type menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link to an ARC">
    Select the ARC this trace belongs to. Linking your trace to an ARC is what builds your milestone progression and keeps your work organized. You can also post a standalone trace if the work doesn't belong to a specific ARC.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write Your Update">
    Describe what you did. Be specific — mention the exact thing you built, fixed, learned, or shipped. Vague updates don't build trust or a following.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Media and Metrics">
    Attach images, a video, or both. Add a number that captures your progress (e.g., "3 new API endpoints," "500 words written," "Day 12 of 30"). Update your milestone progress percentage if applicable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Tap **Publish**. Your trace appears on your profile timeline, in your ARC's trace history, and in the discovery feed for others to find.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Trace Types

Not every update looks the same. Use these types as a guide for what to post and when:

| Trace Type | When to Use It | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily Update** | Regular day-to-day progress logging | "Fixed the auth bug, wrote 3 new tests" |
| **Milestone Hit** | You completed a milestone in your ARC | "Beta version is live — 12 users signed up" |
| **Demo / Preview** | You have something to show | Screen recording of a working feature |
| **Metric Report** | A numbers-focused progress check | "Week 4: 2,100 words, 8 sessions, +200 followers" |
| **Reflection** | A deeper look at what worked and what didn't | "Sprint retrospective: what I'd change next time" |

## How Traces Appear

When you publish a trace, it shows up in three places:

1. **Your Profile Timeline** — All your traces appear in reverse chronological order on your public profile, giving visitors a complete picture of your work history.
2. **Your ARC's Trace History** — Traces linked to an ARC are grouped inside that ARC's detail page, showing your progression on that specific goal.
3. **The Discovery Feed** — Your traces enter the Xera algorithm and can surface to other users based on relevance, recency, and your creator quality score.

<Info>
  Verified builders (Standard plan and above) receive significantly higher feed distribution. Traces from verified accounts are prioritized in the discovery feed algorithm.
</Info>

## Editing and Deleting Traces

You can edit a trace after publishing if you need to fix a typo or add missing information. Open the trace, tap the **⋯** menu, and select **Edit**.

<Warning>
  Editing a trace does not reset its timestamp or feed ranking. However, major changes to published traces may affect how followers and recruiter-viewers perceive your build history — keep edits to corrections, not rewrites.
</Warning>

To delete a trace, open the **⋯** menu and select **Delete**. Deleting a trace removes it from your timeline, your ARC history, and the discovery feed permanently. If the trace was linked to a milestone, your progression percentage may adjust.

<Note>
  Consistency is what builds your Trajectory score — not perfection. Log what you actually did today, even if it was small. A 30-second trace that says "fixed one bug, found two more" is better than nothing. The streak matters more than the size of any single update.
</Note>
